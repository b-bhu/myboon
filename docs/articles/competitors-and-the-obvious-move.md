# Before You Build, Check Who's Already There

*Draft — positioning essay, soft intro to myboon*

---

There's a line widely attributed to Elon Musk, repeated often enough in
business and startup circles that it's worth stating even without a single
clean, citable source:

> "If you're entering anything where there's an existing marketplace,
> against large, entrenched competitors, then your product or service needs
> to be much better than theirs. It can't be a little bit better, because
> then you put yourself in the shoes of the consumer... you're always going
> to buy the trusted brand unless there's a big difference."

Whether or not the wording is exact, the idea underneath it is sound and
worth taking seriously before building anything: don't start with your
product. Start with who's already there, what they've already built, and
why the obvious move hasn't already been made by someone with more
distribution than you. If it has been made, you need to know that going in.

So before talking about what we're building, here's that exercise, done
honestly.

## The industry: mobile crypto apps

To be specific about the space — this isn't "crypto" broadly, and it isn't
trading terminals or desktop dashboards. It's mobile apps that a
crypto-native person actually opens on their phone, day to day, to
understand what's happening in the market and, sometimes, to act on it.

That's a meaningfully narrower category than "crypto," and it's the one
that matters here, because it's where the real competitors already live.

## What we're actually trying to do

Before naming names, it's worth being precise about the goal, because it's
easy to hear "context and action in one app" and assume the pitch is speed
— faster charts, faster execution, a leaner terminal. That's not it.

The goal is to help someone make a *better* decision, not a *faster* one.
Understand what actually moved and why, with enough grounding to trust it,
before deciding whether to act at all. Speed and execution quality are
someone else's game — a deep, well-funded, already-won game, as the next
section shows. Better decisions is the one still open.

## Where the industry stands today

The closest alternative is not one direct competitor. It is the stack a user
assembles today.

**Kaito** focuses on searching and organizing fragmented crypto information.
**Birdeye** focuses on market, token, wallet, and on-chain data. **Jupiter**
brings swaps, perps, lending, and portfolio views into the Solana ecosystem.
**Axiom** combines fast execution with monitoring and trading tools.

These are not bad products making an obvious mistake. They optimize different
parts of the workflow. Information products help users research. Data products
help them inspect. Trading and portfolio products help them act and track. The
user still has to carry the developing story between those surfaces.

That is the remaining opening: not another information destination and not
another trading interface, but the connection between them. Building that
connection requires a research and editorial system as well as the product work
needed to map a story to the relevant asset and venue.

## myboon

myboon is a mobile-first app for crypto markets, built around Solana, built
on that second half.

The loop: a signal moves — a whale bet, a funding spike, an odds shift —
myboon explains what changed and why it matters, you can act on the
relevant market without leaving the app if you want to, and your positions
track what happened next.

The action side — prediction markets, perps, swaps — is necessary
plumbing, not the differentiator. Phantom and Jupiter already proved that
part is table stakes. The bet is entirely on the other half: doing the
research work — checking claims, remembering entities, grading evidence —
before anything gets shown to a user, so what you see is something you can
actually trust, not just something fast.

To be straight about where this stands: myboon is a working beta with no
external users, revenue, or validated demand yet. The research pipeline and
action surfaces exist, and the first direct Story-to-market path connects a
Bitcoin Story to the Phoenix BTC market and places research markers on its
chart. The first user is not "everyone" — it is someone already mobile-first,
already following crypto narratives, and tired of assembling context from
several apps before deciding whether to act.

The obvious move — more trading, bolted onto an existing wallet — already
happened. The harder move — context worth trusting, connected to the
trade — is still open. That's the bet.
