# Weekly — The week that was, the week ahead (approved full script, Bibhu Nov 9, 2026)
Intro (format intro, locked once): "Good evening. This is MyBoon News: the week that was, and the week ahead."
Row 1 (OV, episode-specific): "Looking back, an election that shook the AI trade, one market that went the other way, a regulatory opening for stock perps, and then the dates ahead that will test it all."

We start where the week's story began: Washington. Democrats won both chambers of the incoming Congress.

Investors were already asking whether the huge spending on AI will pay off, and how it's being financed. The election added another question: tighter rules on data centres.

On Thursday, the Nasdaq, the main US tech index, fell more than five and a half percent.

On Friday, the selling reached Asia. South Korea's KOSPI fell about six percent. Samsung dropped more than six. SK hynix, a major maker of AI memory chips, fell more than ten. Japan lost around six percent.

But it wasn't a nonstop crash. US stocks bounced back on Friday. Analysts say several worries were at work, not just one.

One big market went the other way: India.

For the week through Friday, the Nifty 50 and the Sensex, India's two main indices, rose close to nine percent. On Friday alone, they added about two percent each.

Analysts point to three reasons. Indian stocks have less riding on AI spending. Crude oil got cheaper. And foreign investors were buying.

And a separate note from Sunday: in the one-hour Diwali Muhurat session, the Nifty dipped slightly, and the Sensex was almost flat.

Now, a rule change in the US. On November 3rd, the CFTC, the regulator for US futures markets, announced a path for perpetual futures on individual stocks. A "perp" is a contract that tracks a price and never expires. Crypto traders use them every day.

Under the announcement, stock perps could trade on approved US futures exchanges that are also registered with the SEC.

One thing to be clear on: a perp gives you exposure to the price. It doesn't make you a shareholder. And which exchanges will offer them, and when, is still to be seen.

In crypto, all three of the biggest coins fell over the week, though it hasn't closed yet. Bitcoin slipped just over three percent, to about 75,800 dollars. Ether lost just under two percent, to about 2,230. Solana fell the most, about five and a half percent, to roughly 89 dollars.

That was the week that was: a repricing. Next week brings the tests.

Tuesday: SoftBank, one of the biggest backers of AI, reports at 06:30, with a briefing at 07:30. At 13:30, US consumer inflation, the CPI, a key number for interest rates. At 22:00, CoreWeave, which rents out computing power for AI, holds its earnings call, a read on how AI spending is being funded.

Thursday: weekly jobless claims at 13:30. Then at 21:30, Cisco and Applied Materials, which makes chip-making equipment, report. That's a test of real demand for AI hardware.

Friday at 13:30: the PPI, the prices businesses charge each other, one more read on inflation.

Company demand, AI spending and inflation. That's what to watch in the week ahead. This is MyBoon News.
